Every measure, every government

Each cell is the change over that government's years in office, measured from the last year of the government before it. Cells showing a rate (unemployment, inflation) give the average or the change in percentage points, as labelled. A dash means the data does not cover those years.

How the figures are attributed

Each calendar-year figure is assigned to the party in office on July 1 of that year. Each fiscal-year figure (debt, fiscal years ending March 31) is assigned to the party in office on October 1 of the year the fiscal year starts. Handover years therefore go to the party in office at the midpoint. Every row in the tables names the exact baseline and end year it compares, so you can check the choice.

A government's change is measured from the last year assigned to the previous party to the last year assigned to that government. For the first government in a data series, the change starts at the first year the data exists. The current government's figures run to the latest year published.

Dollar figures labelled "real" are adjusted for inflation with the BC Consumer Price Index (2022 dollars) unless the label says otherwise. GDP and GDP per person use Statistics Canada's chained 2017 dollars.

Known gaps and breaks

Debt comes from three BC government publications with slightly different definitions and GDP revisions, so there are small steps where they join (2011/12 to 2012/13, and 2018/19 to 2019/20). The 2025/26 debt figure is an updated forecast, not a final audited actual. Wage data from Statistics Canada ends in 2022 in the table used here. The minimum wage series is currently in constant 2013 dollars and ends in 2013; the nominal rate history and the years after 2013 are not loaded yet.
